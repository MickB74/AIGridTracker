# GridWatch AI — Marketing Plan

*aigridwatch.com · Last updated July 2026*

---

## 1. Positioning

**One-liner:** GridWatch AI is the negotiation toolkit for communities facing a data center proposal — sourced data, model CBA language, and dividend calculators that level the field against a developer's consultants.

**Category:** Not "an AI energy calculator" (crowded, academic). The category to own is **community data-center advocacy** — nobody owns it yet. The watchdog/defender framing from the product vision is the brand.

**Positioning statement:**
> For town councils, citizen groups, and local officials facing a data center proposal, GridWatch AI is the free, open-source watchdog platform that turns 30 days of panic into a negotiation strategy — unlike developer-funded impact studies or generic anti-development activism, every number is sourced and every tool ends in actionable leverage.

**Key differentiators to repeat everywhere:**
1. Every number links to a primary source (EIA, SEC EDGAR, ML.ENERGY, IEA) — credible in a public hearing, not just a Facebook group.
2. Not anti-data-center; pro-negotiation. "Never approve without a CBA" is a stance officials can adopt publicly.
3. Free tools + real deal database (Cedar Rapids, Loudoun County) — proof communities have won before.

**Taglines to test:**
- "Your town has 30 days. The developer has lawyers. Start here."
- "Never approve a data center without a contract."
- "The watchdog for local infrastructure."

## 2. Competitive landscape (researched July 2026)

No competitor covers all three pillars — tracking + negotiation tools + paid community-side advisory — but each pillar now has players, and the tracking space got crowded fast in 2026.

### 2a. Opposition trackers & maps (overlap with the freemium tracker)

| Competitor | What they are | Threat / angle |
|---|---|---|
| [Data Center Watch](https://www.datacenterwatch.org/) | Most-cited tracker; "$64B blocked/delayed" report gets national press | Run by industry-aligned research firm (10a Labs) — frames opposition as risk to manage, opposite of our framing |
| [Data Center Tracker](https://datacentertracker.org/) | Interactive map + legislative action, open database | Closest in shape to our tracker + bills combo |
| [FloridaDataCenters.org](https://floridadatacenters.org/) | Crowdsourced 50-state opposition tracker, 1,100+ reports | Explicitly "stop data centers" activist framing |
| [Data Center Opposition Report](https://datacenteropposition.com/) | Coalition-backed; claims 525K+ members across groups | Organizing reach |
| Erin Brockovich map (brockovichdatacenter.com) | Crowdsourced reporting map, ~4,000 reports within weeks of April 2026 launch | Celebrity awareness we can't match |
| [NAACP Stop Dirty Data Centers](https://naacp.org/campaigns/stop-dirty-data-centers) | National campaign + community reporting system | Organizing infrastructure, EJ credibility |

### 2b. Free CBA/negotiation resources (attack the paid-toolkit layer)

- [Data Center Playbook](https://datacenterplaybook.org/guides.html?guide=citizens) — stakeholder-specific advocacy guides; closest free analog to our toolkit.
- [NAACP CBA template (2026)](https://naacp.org/sites/default/files/documents/NAACP%20CBA%20Template%202026.pdf) — free, credible, downloadable.
- [Good Jobs First](https://goodjobsfirst.org/community-benefit-agreements-with-data-centers-can-help-mitigate-harms/) — the established subsidy watchdog; decades of abatement data, deep journalist/official credibility.
- Think-tank explainers ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/why-community-benefit-agreements-are-necessary-for-data-centers/), [FAS](https://fas.org/publication/community-benefit-agreements-data-center-development/), [Connect Humanity](https://connecthumanity.fund/data-centers-are-coming-heres-how-communities-can-negotiate-for-local-benefit/)) — already rank for our target SEO queries.
- State municipal leagues (e.g. [Michigan Municipal League](https://mml.org/resources-research/data-centers/)) publishing member guidance.

### 2c. Consulting (competes with the advisory tier)

Nearly all expertise serves the **developer side**: [CBRE](https://www.cbre.com/services/property-types/data-center), [Cushman & Wakefield](https://www.cushmanwakefield.com/en/united-states/industries/data-centers), [ICF](https://www.icf.com/work/utilities/data-centers), law firms like [Nelson Mullins](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/services/data-center). Community-side representation is fragmented (local land-use attorneys, ad hoc academics). **No recognized "we sit on the town's side of the table" brand exists — this is the clearest open lane.**

### 2d. AI energy calculators (commoditized — don't compete here)

EcoLogits-based calculators, [Offset AI](https://www.offsetai.app/methodology-transparency-calculations), [AI Cost Lab](https://energy.aicostlab.org/), [OmniCalculator](https://www.omnicalculator.com/ecology/ai-water-footprint), ML.ENERGY itself. The calculator is a feature and a trust signal, not a moat.

### Strategic implications

1. **Own the middle: "negotiate, don't just oppose."** Trackers are either pure opposition or industry-side risk analysis. Nobody occupies sourced-data-in-service-of-a-better-deal — and it's the only positioning a sitting mayor can publicly adopt.
2. **The paid toolkit must beat free.** With NAACP and Data Center Playbook giving templates away, the paid product is the *operational* layer: dollar-figure deal comparables, the dividend calculator with the town's own numbers, the leverage scorecard — things a static template can't do.
3. **Partner with Good Jobs First and municipal leagues** — they have audience and no software; we have tools and no audience yet.
4. **Consulting is the whitespace.** "Community-side data center advisor" has no incumbent brand, and deal sizes justify success fees.

## 3. Audiences (in priority order)

| # | Audience | Pain | Entry point | Converts to |
|---|----------|------|-------------|-------------|
| 1 | **Local officials** — mayors, council members, planning commissioners in rural/mid-size towns | A proposal landed; no staff expertise; election exposure either way | States & officials tab, utility bill explainer | Paid toolkit, consulting |
| 2 | **Citizen organizers** — residents, opposition Facebook groups, "no data center" coalitions | Need hearing-ready facts, not vibes | News/backlash tab, bill impact tools | Newsletter, amplifiers, some toolkit sales |
| 3 | **Local & regional journalists** | Data center stories are hot; hard to source numbers | Deal database, dividend calculator, quotable stats | Backlinks, citations, inbound traffic |
| 4 | **Policy/legal ecosystem** — state legislators' staff, land-use attorneys, academics | Tracking 300+ bills, need comparables | Legislation tracker, methodology page | Credibility, referrals to consulting |

Audience 1 pays the bills. Audiences 2–3 create the demand and the search traffic that finds audience 1.

## 4. Funnel

```
Local news story / search / Reddit thread
        ↓
Free tool (bill calculator, dividend calculator, deal database)
        ↓
Email capture: "Data Center Response Checklist" (PDF lead magnet)
        ↓
Newsletter: weekly digest of deals, bills, and backlash news
        ↓
Paid: Negotiation Toolkit ($) → Consulting engagement ($$$)
```

**Gap to close first:** there is currently no email capture in the app. Ship this before spending effort on traffic — a Streamlit form or Substack embed is enough. Traffic without capture is wasted.

## 5. Channels & tactics

### 5.1 SEO / programmatic content (highest long-term ROI)

The queries people type the week a proposal drops are low-competition and high-intent:

- "[county name] data center proposal"
- "data center tax abatement [state]"
- "how much water does a data center use"
- "will a data center raise my electric bill"
- "community benefit agreement data center template"

**Tactics:**
- Publish one evergreen explainer per core question above (the app content already exists — repackage tabs as indexable pages/posts). Streamlit is weak for SEO; put these on a static blog (even GitHub Pages or Substack) that links into the app.
- **Programmatic state pages:** "Data center legislation in [State], 2026" ×30 states, generated from the bills tracker data. Refresh monthly. These will rank because nobody else has structured data.
- **Deal database as linkbait:** "Every data center community benefit deal in America, with dollar amounts" is the definitive citable resource. Journalists will link to it.

### 5.2 Newsletter (the retention engine)

Weekly "GridWatch Dispatch": 3 community fights this week (from the news feed you already aggregate), 1 deal or bill worth copying, 1 number of the week. The news tab already does the aggregation — the newsletter is 80% built. Substack for discovery, or Buttondown for control.

### 5.3 Reddit & Facebook groups (where the fights happen)

- Monitor (the app already tracks Reddit) subreddits and local Facebook groups where proposals surface. Participate as a helpful expert, not a spammer: answer questions with sourced numbers, link the specific calculator when relevant.
- Rule: value first, link second, never astroturf. One genuinely useful comment in a thread with 500 angry residents outperforms any ad.

### 5.4 Earned media / PR

- **Rapid-response commentary:** when a data center fight makes regional news, email the reporter same-day with a sourced stat and an offer to explain the rate-impact math. Local reporters need exactly this.
- Pitch the deal database and dividend calculator to national outlets already covering the beat (Heatmap, Data Center Dynamics, Business Insider's data center desk, WaPo climate).
- HARO/Qwoted alerts for "data center," "utility rates," "AI energy."

### 5.5 Direct outreach (for consulting revenue)

- Build a watch list from the news feed: every town with a newly reported proposal is a warm lead.
- Send the mayor/council a short, non-salesy email: "Your town was in the news. Here are the three questions to ask [developer] before the next hearing, free. If you want help, we do this on a success-fee basis." Attach the checklist.
- Target: 5–10 of these per week; the news feed makes this a 30-minute task.

### 5.6 Speaking & associations

- National League of Cities, state municipal leagues, APA (planners), NATaT — conference sessions, webinars, and newsletters aimed exactly at audience 1.
- Offer a free 45-minute webinar: "What to ask before you approve a data center." Record it; it becomes evergreen content and a lead magnet.

### 5.7 Open source as marketing

- MIT-licensed, on GitHub — lean into it. "Audit our math" is a trust signal no consultant can match. Encourage academics and journalists to cite the methodology page. Submit to Streamlit gallery, Hacker News (Show HN framed around the data, not the politics).

## 6. 90-day launch sequence

**Phase 1 — Foundation (weeks 1–3)**
- [ ] Email capture + "Data Center Response Checklist" lead magnet in-app

- [ ] Publish the deal database as a public, citable page; pitch it to 10 journalists

- [ ] Show HN + Streamlit gallery submission
- [ ] First webinar for a state municipal league

**Phase 3 — Revenue (weeks 9–13)**

## 8. Risks & guardrails

- **Perceived as anti-tech activism** → always lead with "negotiate, don't just oppose"; feature deals where communities said yes and won concessions.
- **Numbers challenged in a hearing** → methodology page is the shield; never publish an uncited coefficient (already a repo convention).
- **Streamlit SEO ceiling** → accept it; the app is the product, the static blog is the marketing surface.
- **Solo bandwidth** → the news feed automates prospecting; the newsletter reuses the news tab; programmatic pages reuse the bills tracker. Every marketing asset should be generated from data the app already collects.
