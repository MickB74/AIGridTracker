Nvidia Is Guaranteeing $105 Billion for One Data Center Campus in Pike County, Ohio

Nov 18, 2026 · GridWatch AI
Nvidia Ohio Pike County PORTS OpenAI SoftBank financing natural gas transmission AEP Ohio

Nvidia's quarterly filing on Nov. 17 shows the chipmaker standing behind $118 billion in guarantees for other companies' data centers. $105 billion of it backs a single site: the PORTS-Pike campus on the old uranium enrichment plant in Pike County, Ohio. What the filing says, what was promised to the county in August, and the state approvals that are still ahead.

Nvidia reported quarterly results on Nov. 17. The headline numbers were revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier, with $107.6 billion of it from data centers.

The number that matters to a resident is in a different document. The CFO commentary filed with the SEC the same day shows Nvidia has issued guarantees with a maximum exposure of $118.0 billion to help other companies build data centers. Of that, $105.0 billion is credit support for one project: SB Energy's PORTS-Pike Technology Campus in Pike County, Ohio.

A company that sells chips is now backing the land, the power and the buildings. If you live in southern Ohio, that tells you how badly the industry wants this site finished. It does not tell you the site has its approvals.

What the filing says

According to the CFO commentary, as of Oct. 25:

  • $105.0 billion in credit support for SB Energy covers the land, power and building shells for about 4.25 gigawatts across nine buildings at PORTS-Pike. The campus is to host Nvidia hardware under 20-year leases to OpenAI.
  • The guarantees take effect building by building, as each one is complete and ready for service. Nvidia expects the first in its fiscal
  • No payments have been made.
  • There is an option covering roughly 3.8 more gigawatts.
  • A further $9.5 billion is credit support for power and systems, and $3.5 billion guarantees land, power and shells for smaller cloud companies.

On the earnings call, interim chief executive Colette Kress described the guarantees as conditional. As TechCrunch reported, she said a guarantee applies only after the customer has secured its own financing and the facility is built with Nvidia products installed. Data Center Knowledge confirmed the same breakdown of the $118 billion.

So this is not Nvidia writing a $105 billion check to Pike County. Under the terms Nvidia disclosed in August, it is Nvidia promising SB Energy, the landlord, that if a building is finished and OpenAI stops paying, Nvidia will cover the shortfall between a guaranteed minimum lease value and whatever a replacement lease or sale brings in. OpenAI has agreed to reimburse Nvidia. That makes the project easier to finance. It does nothing about permits.

Why chips now come with land and power

The same filing lists what Nvidia has committed to buy from its own suppliers: $390 billion, up from $279 billion a quarter earlier. Chips ordered on that scale need somewhere to run.

A trade press account of the call, from SiliconANGLE, says Kress listed land, power and shell readiness first among the criteria for deciding which customers get chips. The filing also shows $74 billion of data center leases, with Nvidia itself as the tenant, that have not yet started and run up to 20 years.

In plain terms, the constraint on this industry is no longer chips. It is sites with power. That is why a site like the one in Pike County is worth a $105 billion guarantee, and why developers push so hard at the county and state level.

What Pike County was told in August

The project was announced Aug. 17 in a joint release attached to an SEC filing. It describes a campus on and around the decommissioned Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant with:

  • 8 gigawatts of computing load in total, of which the first 4.25 are the part Nvidia is backing.
  • At least 10 gigawatts of new power generation, to be built by SB Energy and SoftBank.
  • At least $4.2 billion in regional grid investment, which the release describes as designed to protect ratepayers. AEP Ohio is the utility.
  • An $80 million community fund, half from SB Energy and half from OpenAI.

OpenAI's own announcement adds that the first 800 megawatts are expected in 2028, largely on existing AEP infrastructure, with construction running through 2032. It says later phases need new power plants, including natural gas, and new transmission lines. It describes cooling as closed-loop and air-cooled, and puts the job count at 35,000 in construction and 2,500 long-term. It says SB Energy will pay the full cost of grid upgrades.

Those are the developers' figures and the developers' promises. For scale, 8 gigawatts of computing load is roughly the output of seven large nuclear reactors, and an $80 million community fund is less than one-tenth of one percent of the $105 billion Nvidia is guaranteeing.

What still needs approval

Trade publication Data Center Frontier reported in August that:

  • About 9.2 gigawatts of the planned generation is natural gas.
  • AEP Ohio's related project is roughly 50 miles of 765-kilovolt transmission line from Pike County to Gallia County, which needs approval from the Ohio Power Siting Board. AEP says the data center customer pays.
  • The project is on a federal fast-track permitting list with the Army Corps of Engineers as lead agency, and Ohio EPA had found a water-quality filing incomplete in July.

We have not re-checked the status of those approvals since August, so treat them as the list of places to look, not as current status. The Ohio Power Siting Board holds public hearings and accepts public comments on transmission lines. A line across two counties affects landowners well outside Pike County.

Questions worth asking in Pike County

  1. Who is on the hook if a building is finished and OpenAI stops paying? Under Nvidia's August disclosure, Nvidia, up to the $105 billion cap. That protects the landlord. Ask what protects the county's tax base and the utility's other customers.
  2. Is the $4.2 billion grid investment a binding condition? A press release is not a tariff. Ask AEP Ohio and the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio where the commitment is written into an approved rate schedule.
  3. What are the terms of the $80 million fund? Who controls it, over how many years, and what happens to it if only the first phase is built.
  4. Which gas plants, and where? More than 9 gigawatts of gas generation means air permits. Each one has a public comment period.

Ohio's governor-elect has said she backs a conditional moratorium, as we covered in our post on the governors' pledges. Nothing reported so far says how that would apply to a project already announced and on a federal permitting track.
