3ERCOT large-load interconnection queue

ERCOT is the only ISO that publishes an aggregate large-load (not generation) interconnection picture and breaks out the data-center share. The story is the funnel: what’s requested dwarfs what’s approved, which dwarfs what’s actually running. Data as of March 26, 2026.

  • Seeking interconnection (410,000 MW) — Total large-load capacity requesting to connect. ~87% is data centers. Mostly speculative — many projects never energize. ERCOT — Large Load Update to Senate Committee on Business & Commerce (Apr 1 2026; data as of Mar 26 2026)
  • Approved to energize (9,042 MW) — Received Approval to Energize from ERCOT Operations — cleared to turn on. ERCOT — Large Load Interconnection status, March TAC report (Mar 13 2026)
  • Actually operational (3,801 MW) — Simultaneous monthly peak ERCOT has actually had to serve — real load on the grid right now (non-simultaneous observed peak: 3,883 MW). ERCOT — Large Load Interconnection status, March TAC report (Mar 13 2026)

5The demand wave — ERCOT & PJM

The two ISOs with the most data-center load growth tell opposite stories about how fast the grid can absorb it.

~233 GWERCOT queue (72.9% DCs)
+32 GWPJM peak-load growth (94% DCs)
23.9 GWDominion zone (+23% vs 2019)

ERCOT has ~233 GW of large-load interconnection requests, 72.9% data centers. Only a fraction will energize — see the funnel above.

PJM projects +32 GW of peak-load growth over the next decade, 94% from data centers. Dominion Energy Virginia’s zone alone shows 23.9 GW of load, up 23% from 2019.

Sources: ERCOT — Large Load Integration (forms, reports & Batch Zero) · PJM — 2025 Long-Term Load Forecast (data-center-driven growth) · EIA — Commercial electricity sales in Virginia driven by data centers (2025)

6How the grid operators & FERC are responding

The through-line: co-location (data centers siting next to power plants to bypass grid queues) and cost allocation (who pays for the transmission upgrades these loads require).

FERC, PJM, ERCOT, MISO & SPP — what each is doing

FERC

In December 2025 FERC directed PJM to write co-location rules for data centers siting next to power plants. In June 2026 FERC issued show-cause orders to MISO, SPP, and other RTOs requiring them to adopt large-load interconnection standards. FERC — directs PJM to write co-location rules for data centers (Dec 18, 2025 fact sheet; Docket EL25-49/AD24-11) · FERC — show-cause orders to MISO, SPP & other RTOs on large-load interconnection (Jun 18, 2026)

PJM

The 2025 capacity auction cleared at record prices (~$16.4B total across 13 states), driven in large part by data-center load growth. PJM is developing new co-location rules per FERC’s order. Monitoring Analytics (PJM market monitor) — data centers drove $6.3B of the record $16.4B 2025 capacity auction (38%), and $29.4B of $63.6B across the last four auctions (46%); see the 2025/2026 RPM Base Residual Auction analyses

ERCOT / Texas SB 6

Texas SB 6 (signed 2025) authorizes ERCOT to curtail or disconnect large loads (≥75 MW) in emergencies, establishes new interconnection standards, and imposes a $50,000/MW fee on new large-load interconnections. Texas SB 6 (89R, signed June 20, 2025) — ERCOT may curtail/disconnect large loads (≥75 MW) in emergencies; new interconnection standards. Official bill text & history · Texas Legislature — SB 6 (89R), large-load grid rules, signed June 20, 2025. Official bill text & history

MISO

MISO launched its Large Load Interconnection Reliability (LLIR) process, requiring 90-day reliability studies for loads ≥100 MW before interconnection. MISO — Large Load Additions & Large Load Interconnection Reliability Requirements (2025–26)

SPP

SPP adopted its High Impact Large Load (HILL) 90-day study process for loads that would materially change system conditions. SPP — High Impact Large Load (HILL) integration, the 90-day study process from RR696; FERC accepted the tariff revisions effective Jan 15 2026 (first-party)
