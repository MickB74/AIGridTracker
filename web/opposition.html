Know the opposition

Industry lobby playbook

9 organizations, 10 documented claims, and the sourced counter-arguments you need at the hearing. These are the groups shaping the narrative around data center development — know their arguments before you walk into the room.

⚠️ Data Center Coalition (DCC) Primary lobby

https://datacentercoalition.org

Reports & Publications · 2026 PwC impact study

Revenue tripled from $583K (2022) to $2.5M (2023). Operates Virginia Connects 501(c)(4) running national-security-themed video ads.

Industry claim: Data centers contribute $927 billion to GDP, support 5.5 million jobs, and generate $204 billion in tax revenue. [source]

Counter: The 5.5M figure uses indirect and induced multiplier models that count any dollar touched by the supply chain. A typical 100 MW campus employs 50–75 full-time workers. The PwC study was commissioned and paid for by the DCC itself.

Industry claim: Data centers do not raise residential electricity rates. [source]

Counter: The E3 report (May 2026) was commissioned by the DCC. Independent analyses by the Brattle Group and state PUCs in Virginia, Georgia, and the Carolinas have found data center load growth contributes to rate pressure through transmission and generation upgrades that enter the rate base.

⚠️ Chamber of Progress Primary lobby

https://progresschamber.org

Loudoun tax analysis · NY moratorium veto letter

Funded by Google, Amazon, Meta, Apple, and other tech giants. Positions itself as center-left.

Industry claim: Without data centers, Loudoun County homeowners would pay ~$5,800 more per year in property taxes. [source]

Counter: Loudoun's model is the exception, not the rule — most jurisdictions grant abatements that waive most of the tax revenue data centers would otherwise generate. Even in Loudoun, the county chose not to offer abatements; the Chamber's counterfactual assumes the entire industry vanishes overnight, not that it pays less.

⚠️ TechNet Primary lobby

https://technet.org

2026 State Policy Principles (PDF) · State policy agenda

Lobbied on 808 bills across all 50 states in 2025, with a 87% win rate on their policy positions.

Industry claim: Data centers deserve sales and use tax exemptions, as is done for manufacturing. [source]

Counter: Unlike manufacturing plants, data centers employ very few people per dollar invested. A 100 MW campus may represent $1–2 billion in investment but create only 50–75 permanent jobs — orders of magnitude fewer per dollar than a factory receiving comparable incentives.

🌳 Clean Energy Buyers Association (CEBA) Green cover

https://ceba.org

2026 State of the Market · Deal Tracker

Industry claim: Corporate buyers have signed 143 GW of clean energy deals since 2014. [source]

Counter: PPAs are contracts to purchase output, not proof of additionality. Many cover existing projects that would have been built anyway. CEBA’s own data shows the number of active buyers dropped 40% year-over-year — the market is concentrating among a handful of hyperscalers, not broadening.

🏛️ Information Technology Industry Council (ITI) Policy & technical authority

https://itic.org

Energy policy · Data center Q&A

Affiliate 'The Green Grid' promotes PUE as self-regulation.

Industry claim: Data centers are critical national infrastructure requiring streamlined permitting. [source]

Counter: The “critical infrastructure” framing is used to bypass local environmental and planning review. Data centers are private commercial facilities; the data they store could be distributed across existing infrastructure. Streamlined permitting has meant communities lose their window to negotiate.

Industry claim: PUE (Power Usage Effectiveness) proves data centers are efficient. [source]

Counter: PUE only measures how efficiently a building uses power for computing vs. cooling. It says nothing about absolute energy consumption, water use, grid impact, or community burden. Uptime Institute found only 23% of operators report all three scopes of carbon emissions.

🤝 7x24 Exchange Community engagement

https://7x24exchange.org

Art of Being a Good Neighbor · International Data Center Day

Industry claim: Data centers are good neighbors who engage with communities proactively. [source]

Counter: The 10-step playbook focuses on PR and workforce pipeline (school hackathons, “Cloud Run” festivals) rather than binding commitments on noise, water, or tax revenue. Voluntary “good neighbor” pledges have no enforcement mechanism.

📍 Consumer Energy Alliance (CEA) State-level / commissioned

https://consumerenergyalliance.org

Economic Benefits of Data Centers (PDF)

Industry-funded; board includes fossil fuel and utility executives.

Industry claim: Data centers bring economic benefits to communities including jobs, investment, and infrastructure improvements. [source]

Counter: CEA is an industry-funded group whose board includes fossil fuel and utility executives. Their economic impact studies do not account for opportunity costs: what else that land, water, and grid capacity could have supported.

📊 Uptime Institute Technical authority

https://uptimeinstitute.com

Sustainability Manager's Guide

Not directly community-facing, but useful counterpoint: their own survey found only 23% of operators report all three scopes of carbon emissions, and only 41% track water usage.

📍 Northern Virginia Technology Council (NVTC) State-level / commissioned

https://nvtc.org

Won TECNA's Public Policy Advocacy Award for tax incentive lobbying.

Industry claim: Data centers are the backbone of Northern Virginia's economy.

Counter: NVTC publishes biennial economic impact studies commissioned by the industry. Won TECNA’s Public Policy Advocacy Award for lobbying on data center tax incentives.

The playbook at a glance

Six core industry claims to prepare for:

  1. Jobs — Headline figures (5.5M) use indirect/induced multipliers. Ask for permanent on-site FTEs, not construction or supply-chain estimates.
  2. Tax revenue — Often paired with abatements that waive most of the revenue. Ask for the net after incentives.
  3. Electricity rates — "We don’t raise rates" is based on a DCC-commissioned study. PUC filings in multiple states show transmission upgrades entering the rate base.
  4. National security — The "critical infrastructure" frame is used to bypass local review. Data centers are private commercial facilities.
  5. Clean energy — PPA announcements are not the same as additionality. Buyer concentration is rising, not broadening.
  6. Self-regulation — PUE measures building efficiency, not absolute impact. Only 23% of operators report all three scopes of emissions.
