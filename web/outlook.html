How much electricity will data centers actually use?

The global and US forecasts, side by side — and why credible forecasters disagree by a factor of three.

  • AI's slice of data-center power is projected to climb from 5–15% recently to 35–50% by 2030.
  • Inference dominates. It accounts for the majority of a model's lifetime energy — over 90% by some operator accounts. Usage, not training, is the lever.
  • Jevons paradox. Per-query efficiency keeps improving (Gemini fell ~33× in a year), but cheaper inference drives more usage, so total load still rises.

  • In capacity terms: BloombergNEF sees US data-center power reaching ~106 GW by 2035, up from ~25 GW in 2024 — 8.6% of all US electricity, more than double today's 3.5%. BloombergNEF — US data-center power demand ~106 GW by 2035 (first-party insight, Dec 2025)
  • Why the spread: forecasts hinge on how much announced pipeline actually gets built and powered — interconnection queues are heavily speculative — plus efficiency gains and utilisation assumptions.

Forecasters: IEA, Energy and AI (2025) + Key Questions update (2026) · BloombergNEF (BNEF) — data-center power-demand research & forecasts · Gartner — data-center electricity to double by 2030 (~980 TWh) · S&P Global / 451 Research — global data-center demand ~1,587 TWh by 2030 · EPRI — Powering Intelligence 2026 (US Low/Medium/High scenarios) · Lawrence Berkeley National Lab, 2024 US Data Center Energy Usage Report — 325–580 TWh (6.7–12% of US electricity) by 2028, up from 176 TWh (4.4%) in 2023 · World Resources Institute — US 2030 forecasts span 206–970 TWh

If a developer cites a single forecast at your hearing, ask which one and why. There is no consensus number, and the choice between 350 TWh and 970 TWh is doing a lot of work in any argument about whether the grid can absorb a new campus.

The rural migration

Pew Research Center analysed where new US facilities are going (April 2026). The finding that matters for community advocacy: the build-out has moved to rural counties, most of which have never hosted a data center and have no zoning precedent for one.

67%of planned facilities are rural — against 13% of those operating today
39%are landing in counties with zero current data centers
42%of Americans will live within 5 miles of one, up from 38%
  • Regional drivers: the South and Midwest are capturing three-quarters of all planned US developments. The South alone accounts for 48% of upcoming sites.
  • Growth speed: planned developments represent a 62% increase in total facilities for the South and 64% for the Midwest, relative to current counts.
  • Tight clusters: 90% of all operating and planned sites are within 5 miles of another. The first approval in a county is rarely the last.

Why this lands on you: a county that has never permitted a data center has no template — no zoning overlay, no noise ordinance, no water study, and often no staff who have read one of these applications before. That is the gap the toolkit is built for.
