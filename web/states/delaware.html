The record in short: Coons introduced the Liquid Cooling for AI Act in November 2025 to have GAO and DOE assess liquid cooling for data centers, saying AI leadership "shouldn't have to mean skyrocketing energy bills for American families." In September 2026 he voted against advancing the House-passed Ratepayer Protection Act; no statement from him explaining that vote was found. In November 2026 he voted for the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, a permitting bill he said ensures data centers pay their fair share and pay for upgrades to electricity systems.

What they have done — bills, votes, letters, formal acts

  • Voted for Senate passage of the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026 (H.R. 2347 as amended; Senate vote 270, 62-33) and said it would ensure data centers pay more than their fair share in the communities they impact. He said he had fought unsuccessfully for amendments to the bill's environmental and historic-preservation provisions. (2026-11-19 — Sen. Coons press release)
  • Voted Nay on cloture on the motion to proceed to H.R. 9340, the House-passed Ratepayer Protection Act (Senate vote 254); the motion was rejected 57-43. (2026-09-30 — U.S. Senate roll call vote 254 (119th Congress, 2nd session))
  • Introduced the Liquid Cooling for AI Act with Sen. Dave McCormick: GAO to assess R&D needs for liquid cooling in data centers, DOE to evaluate the findings and report recommendations to Congress, and a liquid cooling advisory organization of industry experts. The release cites a Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory report that data centers used 4.4% of U.S. electricity in 2023 and could reach 12.8% by 2028. (2025-11-20 — Sen. Coons press release)

What they have said — promises, not actions

  • Announced he would support the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026, a permitting bill, saying it ensures data centers pay their fair share and pay for upgrades to electricity systems; said he expected an amendment process to improve it. Senate vote 257 the same day (cloture on the motion to proceed to H.R. 2347, 73-25) records him as Yea. (2026-11-09 — Sen. Coons press release)
