6Case studies
Mixed outcome

The Dalles, OR — Google funded the water system — and its share of the water kept climbing

Under a 2021 agreement Google paid roughly $28.5M toward upgrades to The Dalles' water treatment and storage, including an aquifer storage and recovery system it later handed to the city, and bought and donated 3.88 million gallons/day of water rights from a closed aluminium smelter. What the deal did not include was a cap on Google's own draw. When The Oregonian sued for the usage records, the city spent 13 months fighting disclosure before settling — and the records showed the campus using about 29% of the city's water, rising to roughly 40% (some 550 million gallons a year) by 2025. Infrastructure money is not the same protection as a volume limit. This is the case that shows the difference, and the reason to put a cap in writing.

7What Oregon's own study found

Data Centers and Energy Use in Oregon (2024)
Oregon Department of Energy (ODOE) Sector Report

Statewide review of energy draw, water rights in The Dalles (Columbia River), and utility disclosure laws.

Columbia River / AquiferWater Source
BPA Hydropower + Mixed GridEnergy Source
Improved (New state disclosure laws)Transparency Level
Water rights vs. Agricultural demandPrimary Issue
  • Columbia River Water Draw: Spotlights Google's major campus at The Dalles. Evaporative cooling draws significant water from the municipal aquifer, causing municipal water rights disputes during dry seasons.
  • Hydroelectric Load: Historically attracted by cheap Bonneville Power Administration (BPA) hydropower. However, hydro capacity is fully allocated, forcing new expansion to draw from mixed Pacific Northwest grids with higher carbon intensities.
  • Transparency Laws: Led to new state laws regarding municipal utility disclosure. Utilities are now permitted to release actual water consumption figures, which were previously protected as proprietary trade secrets.
