The seven documents worth asking for

Every project page ends with Find the paper trail — the registers, dockets and queues that hold that project's record. This is what to look for once you are there, roughly in the order the documents tend to exist. A records request that arrives after the vote is worth nothing, so ask early and ask by name.

Air permit for the backup generators

Who holds it: State environmental agency (air quality division)

Plan approval / construction permit for the diesel or gas gensets, with the emissions inventory and run-hour limits.

Why it is worth having: The genset count and permitted run hours are the most reliable public proxy for the campus's real size — often filed before any MW figure is public, and usually larger than what the developer told the town.

Water withdrawal / supply agreement

Who holds it: State water authority, river basin commission, or the municipal water utility

Permitted withdrawal volume, the source, and any will-serve letter from the water utility.

Why it is worth having: Turns 'we use very little water' into a number on letterhead, and shows whether existing customers were modelled during a drought year.

NPDES / stormwater and discharge permits

Who holds it: State environmental agency (water division) or EPA region

Construction stormwater permit, erosion plan, and any cooling-blowdown discharge authorisation.

Why it is worth having: Earliest filing on most sites — often the first hard proof of acreage and grading before a rezoning is even docketed.

Rezoning / special-use application file

Who holds it: County or town planning department

The full application: site plan, traffic study, noise study, proffers, staff report, and every submitted revision.

Why it is worth having: The staff report names the conditions staff thought were necessary. Conditions dropped between versions are the single most productive thing to read aloud at a hearing.

Interconnection / large-load service request

Who holds it: Grid operator (RTO/ISO) and the serving utility

Queue position, requested MW, in-service date, and any network upgrades the project triggers.

Why it is worth having: Shows the real megawatts and who pays for the wires. A queue request far larger than the public figure is a phased build the community has not been told about.

Special contract / rate filing at the PUC

Who holds it: State public utility commission docket

Any special tariff, large-load class, or contract the utility filed to serve this customer, plus its cost allocation.

Why it is worth having: This is where it becomes visible whether other ratepayers subsidise the connection. Testimony and exhibits are public and usually more candid than the press release.

Tax abatement / incentive agreement

Who holds it: County, school district, IDA or state development authority

The PILOT or abatement resolution, the jobs and investment commitments, and any clawback terms.

Why it is worth having: Converts 'economic development' into the actual net revenue per year, and shows what happens if the promised jobs never appear.

These are places to look, not evidence that anything has been filed. Links were checked on 2026-08-21; search links are queries, not records.
