PVI R+5 · Seat held by Jon Husted (Republican) · Interim appointee nominated

At stake in this state: 203 data centers · 7.5 TWh/year · 18 local moratoriums in force

Jon Husted (Republican) · incumbent Mixed

Frames AI buildout as a national-security necessity, and introduced a bill directing states to make 100 MW+ loads cover their own infrastructure costs.

  • Introduced the Ratepayer Protection Act: standards for states and PUCs connecting 100 MW+ loads, requiring large-load customers to recover full incremental upgrade costs and post financial assurances so utilities cannot shift them to households. “We must do that without passing the costs on to working families and small businesses.” (2026-07-20 — source)
  • Bill introduced the day after Brown's first attack ad aired; reported to have taken roughly $69,750 from tech and energy PACs and employees including Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Oracle, AEP, Duke Energy and Vistra. (2026-08-21 — source)

How this page is built

The ballot roster is primary-sourced. Every race links to the state election authority's own certified candidate list. Who is on the ballot is a fact the state publishes, so that is where it is read from — not from a wire round-up.

Every claim carries its own link and date. A candidate's position is a claim someone may repeat at a hearing or in a letter, so it ships with the source that backs it and the date we read it.

Silence is disclosed, never scored. “Lean” summarises only the cited items below it, on one axis: does this candidate's documented position make data centers pay their own way and give the host community a say? It is not an overall rating, not a grade, and not an endorsement. Candidates with no located record are left unscored.

This is not the officials scorecard. The A–F scorecard grades sitting officials on roll-call votes and signed laws. Most people here are challengers whose whole record is a campaign statement — much weaker evidence. A press release is a promise, not an action, and this page will not launder one into the other, which is why nobody here gets a letter grade.

Tech & utility campaign money

Where a money line appears under a candidate, it counts committee (PAC) receipts reported to the FEC whose contributing-committee name matches a published term list — the list ships in scripts/fetch_senate_money.py, and every matched committee is written into the data file, so any figure here can be audited back to the committees behind it. It undercounts by design: individual contributions from company employees are not included, because the FEC publishes no reliable employer-to-industry rollup. Read any total as a floor. Figures quoted inside a candidate's record items above come from the linked reporting instead, and are attributed there.

As of August 2026 · changes weekly — re-check before citing

The ballot roster is read from each state's own certified candidate list and is solid; the AI/data-center records are not evenly covered. Most filed candidates have no located record and are shown as such rather than as neutral. Rosters also still move — a pending primary, a withdrawal, or a court ruling can change the field, so check the race's linked state candidate list before quoting a name.

Lean labels

  • Guardrails — Documented support for making data centers carry their own costs, for community say over siting, or for a pause.
  • Mixed — Documented on both sides — courts the buildout but backs some ratepayer or community protection.
  • Accelerate — Documented emphasis on building faster — competitiveness, incentives, or siting help — without a matching cost or community safeguard on the record.
  • No record found — No documented statement or action on data centers located. Not scored — the gap is disclosed, not punished.
