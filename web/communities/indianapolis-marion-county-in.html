The data center fight in Indianapolis (Marion County), Indiana

As of September 5, 2026, Indianapolis (Marion County), Indiana has a data center moratorium in force (action taken Aug 19, 2026; documented end date 2027-12-31).

Status is derived, not stored — a pause with a documented end date flips to Expired on its own — and the source is right next to the claim.

Council President Maggie Lewis (District 5) authored and sponsored the moratorium, announcing it Jul 10 and introducing it to committee Jul 13. The City-County Council advanced it 23-1 on Aug 10 (lone no: Michael-Paul Hart, District 20, whose district includes the DC Blox site) and the Metropolitan Development Commission gave final 6-0 approval Aug 19 as a zoning-ordinance amendment, through Dec 31, 2027. State law protects three already-approved projects, which are NOT paused — DC Blox (Warren Twp), Metrobloks (Martindale-Brightwood) and Sabey (Decatur Twp). A committee will study campus size, building height, environmental impact and sound levels before the pause lifts. Marion County and Indianapolis are one jurisdiction under Unigov

Two-building, 900,000+ sq ft, $4B campus near Camby Rd/Kentucky Ave. Approved by Metropolitan Development Commission in March 2026 via industrial-zoning adjustment; ~90 residents protested. Residents later sued; case helped drive Indianapolis's data-center moratorium push.

Metrobloks Martindale-Brightwood — Approved

Operator: Metrobloks · Owner: Metrobloks · Acres: 14

$500M, two-building, ~168,000 sq ft data-center campus at 2505 N. Sherman Ave. on the site of a long-abandoned drive-in theater, in a neighborhood with a documented history of lead-contaminated soil. Uses a closed-loop cooling system (water added once, then recirculated) and buys power from AES Indiana rather than the residential grid. MDC approved 3 variances + a rezoning 6-2 on April 1, 2026; City-County Council gave final rezoning approval May 4, 2026. Exempted from the Aug. 19, 2026 citywide moratorium as a previously-approved project. Residents' group Protect Martindale Brightwood said it was exploring legal options to challenge the approval. District councilor Ron Gibson's home was shot 13 times on April 7, 2026, days after he publicly backed the project; a note reading 'No Data Centers' was left at the scene.

Next: Approved — pivot from stopping it to a binding CBA: enforceable caps on noise, water and cost, plus a community benefit.

Where the decision gets made

Indianapolis-Marion County City-County Council (moratorium ordinance sponsor and final legislative vote), with the Metropolitan Development Commission (MDC) as the zoning body giving final regulatory approval; consolidated city-county government (Unigov). — Zoning and rezoning ordinances, including the data-center moratorium and any future data-center-specific zoning rules, for the City of Indianapolis / all of Marion County (minus the four excluded municipalities).

Meets: Full Council typically meets monthly, generally the second Monday of the month; committees (including the Metropolitan and Economic Development Committee, which handled the moratorium) meet on their own monthly schedule -- see the live 2026 Council Meeting Calendar for exact dates. The Metropolitan Development Commission's hearing examiner holds petition hearings on the 2nd and 4th Thursday of the month at 1 p.m.; the full nine-member MDC meets separately to ratify or hear appeals of hearing-examiner decisions.
Where: City-County Building, Beurt R. SerVaas Public Assembly Room, 200 E. Washington St., Indianapolis, IN 46204

Public comment: Residents can email PlannerOnCall@Indy.gov on a rezoning/variance petition, testify at MDC hearing-examiner sessions (15-minute developer presentation, comparable time for opponents, ~5-minute rebuttals), or contact councilors individually or via city-county.council@indy.gov. A single district councilor can pull a rezoning item for individual floor discussion; blocking a proposal on the floor requires 15 of 25 councilor votes against it.
