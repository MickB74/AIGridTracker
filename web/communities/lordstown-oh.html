Latest in the record

  • 2026-06-16 — Lordstown Village Council approved by emergency reading a 180-day extension of the data center moratorium, effective July 5, 2026 through January 2, 2027, to allow continued development of zoning standards for setbacks, noise, water usage, and other data center impacts. · source
  • 2026-05-05 — Lordstown council approved a consulting agreement with planner Brian Frantz (not to exceed $7,625) for zoning and data center matters and advertised a public hearing to extend the data center moratorium for an additional 180 days. · source
  • 2026-03-25 — Lordstown officials heard from land-use planner Brian Frantz, who advised the village to define data centers in zoning codes, designate appropriate locations, and use conditional-use permits to control placement, design, noise, and lighting. · source
  • 2026-01-05 — Lordstown Village Council unanimously approved a 180-day moratorium on new data center permits after a public hearing attended by more than 50 residents, most of whom spoke against data centers; the moratorium was set to expire July 4, 2026. · source
  • 2025-12-24 — The Ohio Supreme Court issued an entry denying Lordstown's motion to dismiss the Bristolville 25 mandamus case against the village and its zoning inspector, while granting dismissal of the village solicitor as a party; the court also granted an alternative writ. · source
  • 2025-12-01 — Lordstown Village Council repealed its data center ban on the advice of the village solicitor, who stated Ohio courts favor moratoriums over outright bans; a public hearing on a replacement 180-day moratorium was scheduled for January 5, 2026. · source

Illustrative rather than exhaustive — dozens of localities churn weekly, and this tracks the ones we have read a source for. **Each row carries its own provenance.** Rows showing a verification date link to the ordinance, the enacting body's own page, or a datable report of the vote, and were read on that date. Rows marked *unverified* came from bulk compilation and have not been checked against a primary source — the locality, the year, and the status may all be off. Verify one of those with the town clerk before citing it as precedent. **Expiry is derived, not asserted.** A row with a documented end date flips to *Expired* on its own once that date passes. A time-limited moratorium whose start date was never documented keeps showing as enacted — that is a known gap, not a claim that it is still in force. Moratoriums are also routinely extended, so an expiry date is the earliest it could have ended, not proof that it did.
