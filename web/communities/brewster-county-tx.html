The project on the table

Pacifico Energy Glass Mountains data center and gas plant (proposed) — Proposed

Operator: Pacifico Energy · Acres: 8,400

Pacifico Energy, which is developing the GW Ranch gas plant for Amazon's Pecos County campus, presented Brewster County Commissioners Court with a concept for an off-grid data center powered by gas turbines (possibly with solar and batteries) on a roughly 8,400-acre ranch in the Glass Mountains owned by James Ahern. No filing, tenant, size or development agreement yet; the company's own cost range is $5 billion to $60 billion.

Next: Early stage. File a public-records request for the application and site plan.

Latest in the record

  • 2026-09-18 — Marfa Public Radio reported the company described an off-grid data center and gas plant employing 100 to 200 people, possibly with worker housing, costing $5 billion to $60 billion, with one to two years of planning before construction. County Judge Greg Henington said 'we're not taking this, like a lay down' and raised water and noise; about 10 residents spoke against it over air quality, noise, dark skies, housing and the region's identity. · source

Where the decision gets made

Commissioners Court — County budget, roads and any tax abatement or development agreement for unincorporated Brewster County; a county judge and four precinct commissioners. Texas counties generally lack zoning power over unincorporated land, so an abatement or development agreement is where conditions on a project get written. Pacifico Energy, developer of the gas plant powering Amazon's Pecos County campus, presented an off-grid data center and gas plant concept for a Glass Mountains ranch at a special meeting on Sep 15, 2026 (no action taken).

Meets: 2nd and 4th Tuesdays, 9:30 a.m. Central (special meetings posted separately); broadcast live on the county YouTube channel
Where: Commissioners Courtroom, Brewster County Courthouse, 201 West Avenue E, Alpine, TX 79830

Public comment: Every agenda opens with public comment, limited to 5 minutes per person; the public can also comment by phone at 432-244-6663. The county posts a public comment sheet and its rules of conduct on the Commissioners Court page.
