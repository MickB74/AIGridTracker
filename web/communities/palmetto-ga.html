The project on the table

T5@ATL IV (Palmetto) — In review

Operator: T5 Data Centers · Size: 300 MW · Acres: 91

New multi-building Atlanta-area campus scalable to 300MW with on-site substation, expected operational in 2026.

Next: An application is in the record. Ask the planning office for the next hearing date and request formal notice.

Latest in the record

  • 2026-03-01 — The Palmetto City Council unanimously approved a rewritten industrial zoning code that permanently excludes data centers from the city, following community concern that had built up around the T5 project and area data-center growth; Mayor Teresa Thomas-Smith cited an "overall disinterest" in the warehouse-like structures. · source
  • 2026-01-07 — The City of Palmetto held a special city council session that advanced discussion of a potential economic development agreement tied to the T5 project. · source
  • 2025-11-01 — The Atlanta Regional Commission completed its Development of Regional Impact review of the revised ATL IV application, a milestone reported as clearing the way toward construction; the Fairburn portion of the site already held full zoning/permits under existing industrial designation. · source
  • 2025-05-05 — T5 submitted a revised, scaled-down DRI application for ATL IV consisting of three data center buildings totaling up to 1,320,000 sq ft along Gullatt Road, with supporting access roads, parking, utilities, and stormwater infrastructure. · source
  • 2024-08-28 — T5 finalized acquisition of the 91-acre Palmetto/South Fulton County site and publicly announced the ATL IV campus, describing it as scalable up to 300MW with an on-site substation targeted for 2026 operation and liquid cooling to support AI workloads. · source
  • 2024-07-16 — T5 withdrew its original DRI application, which by that point had grown to a proposed 2.95 million-sq-ft, 300MW campus across seven buildings. · source

Illustrative rather than exhaustive — dozens of localities churn weekly, and this tracks the ones we have read a source for. **Each row carries its own provenance.** Rows showing a verification date link to the ordinance, the enacting body's own page, or a datable report of the vote, and were read on that date. Rows marked *unverified* came from bulk compilation and have not been checked against a primary source — the locality, the year, and the status may all be off. Verify one of those with the town clerk before citing it as precedent. **Expiry is derived, not asserted.** A row with a documented end date flips to *Expired* on its own once that date passes. A time-limited moratorium whose start date was never documented keeps showing as enacted — that is a known gap, not a claim that it is still in force. Moratoriums are also routinely extended, so an expiry date is the earliest it could have ended, not proof that it did.
