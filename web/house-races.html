How this page is built, and how it stays current

The ballot is primary-sourced. Every district links the state election authority's own certified candidate list. 435 voting districts plus 5 non-voting delegates, and where mid-decade redistricting put two sitting members in one seat, both are shown.

Every claim carries its own link and date, and a record older than 120 days is flagged as needing a re-read rather than shown as current.

Silence is disclosed, never scored. “Lean” summarises only the cited items below it, on one axis: does this candidate's documented position make data centers pay their own way and give the host community a say? It is not an overall rating, not a grade, and not an endorsement. Candidates with no located record are left unscored.

Records are promoted by a human, never by a script. scripts/scan_candidate_records.py mines the news archive for a candidate's name alongside data-center terms and files what it finds into data/candidate_record_candidates.json. It never writes this registry — promotion is where source and as_of come from, and those are the two fields that make a claim quotable at a hearing. scripts/verify_candidate_records.py then re-checks every published link and flags what has gone stale or dead.

“Recent mentions” are not records. Where a card shows them, they are unreviewed headlines matched by script — the same bargain the story tracker already makes. They are leads for the queue, and the page says so on every block.

Lean labels

  • Guardrails — Documented support for making data centers carry their own costs, for community say over siting, or for a pause.
  • Mixed — Documented on both sides — courts the buildout but backs some ratepayer or community protection.
  • Accelerate — Documented emphasis on building faster — competitiveness, incentives, or siting help — without a matching cost or community safeguard on the record.
  • No record found — No documented statement or action on data centers located. Not scored — the gap is disclosed, not punished.
