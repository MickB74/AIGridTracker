What has happened so far (10 events)

  • 2019-01-02coverage
    Data Center Dynamics reports that a project code-named 'Project Wizard' in Sarpy County has evidence pointing to Google as the developer of a major new data center. · source
  • 2019-10-04announcement
    Google holds a groundbreaking ceremony for a $600 million data center on a 275-acre site off Highway 50 and Schram Road in Papillion, with Governor Pete Ricketts, Congressman Don Bacon, and Mayor David Black in attendance; the facility is described as Google's 13th nationwide. · source
  • 2019-10-04construction
    KMTV reports that OPPD had been collaborating with Google on power infrastructure for approximately six years prior to the groundbreaking, and Google expects the Papillion facility to be operational within 18-24 months. · source
  • 2022-04-21announcement
    Google announces at least $750 million in new Nebraska investment including expansion of its Papillion campus at 14706 Schram Road (then employing 120 people) and a new data center in northwest Omaha, bringing total state investment past $1 billion. · source
  • 2023-08-22announcement
    Google announces $1.2 billion in new Nebraska investment to expand the Papillion and Omaha facilities and build a third data center in Lincoln; the Papillion facility is described as having created over 120 jobs since opening in 2019. · source
  • 2025-11-18coverage
    In November 2025, Silicon Prairie News reports that OPPD now anticipates annual electricity demand growth of over 100 MW (up from 4 MW previously), driven by data center expansion including Google's Sarpy County facility described as its first in Nebraska. · source
  • 2026-06-03environmental
    Google announces $17 million in water stewardship grants across seven states; the Metropolitan Utilities District in Omaha receives $3 million for acoustic leak-detection sensors across 500 miles of water lines, tied to Google's three Nebraska data centers. · source
  • 2026-07-08coverage
    In July 2026, Flatwater Free Press reports that Google's Nebraska data centers consumed approximately 732 million gallons of water in 2025, up from 46.6 million gallons in 2022 and 134.7 million gallons in 2023; the Papillion facility is identified as one of three sites. · source
  • 2026-07-15report
    Grist reports that Nebraska passed a law requiring data centers to disclose annual water consumption and power demand to the Department of Water, Energy, and Environment; Google's three Nebraska facilities including Papillion consumed approximately 732 million gallons in 2025. · source
  • 2026-07-20incentive
    Governor Pillen signs an executive order curbing tax incentives for large data centers; KOLN reports that Nebraska data centers have received $519 million in property tax exemptions since 2021. · source

Find the paper trail

Find the paper trail (0 filed · 11 places to look)

State environmental permits

Backup generators, water withdrawal and stormwater are permitted by the state, usually earlier and in more detail than anything filed locally.

  • Nebraska Dept. of Environment & EnergyThe program page: which permits this state requires, and the office that issues them.
  • Search Nebraska Dept. of Environment & Energy for this projectsearchThis state publishes no searchable permit database, so a written records request to the agency is the reliable route. Use this search only to find the right office.

County & town filings

The rezoning application, site plan, traffic and noise studies, staff report and every revision.

  • Search Papillion planning filingssearchPlanning departments post application files under many different names. Find the department, then ask the clerk for the application number — that number unlocks everything else.
  • Search Papillion property records / GISsearchThe parcel record names the buying LLC and the sale date, which is what ties an anonymous filing to an operator.

Utility & grid records

The electricity request is filed with the grid operator and the utility long before the town hears about it — and states the real load.

  • Southwest Power Pool (Plains) — interconnection queueRequested megawatts, queue position and target in-service date. The MW here is the real number; the one at the town meeting is often phase one.
  • Nebraska Power Review Board — docket searchSpecial contracts, large-load tariffs and rate cases are docketed here. Testimony and exhibits are public, and are usually more candid than the press release.
  • FERC eLibraryFederal filings: transmission agreements, interconnection service agreements, and co-location disputes.
  • Search the serving utility for a will-serve lettersearchThe will-serve or load-service letter is the utility's own written commitment. Ask the utility and the PUC for it by name — it is frequently the first document to exist.

Federal databases

Works anywhere, and covers the operator's record at its other sites.

  • EPA ECHO facility searchEvery federally-reported air, water and waste permit at a facility, plus its inspection and violation history. Search by address or draw a radius around the site. This project's locality centres on 41.150, -96.040 — search that point with a 5-mile radius.
  • EPA EnvirofactsThe same underlying records, queryable by county — useful for 'what else is permitted near this site' rather than one facility.
  • EPA Facility Registry ServiceResolves the shell LLC on a permit to the facility it belongs to. Worth a look when the filing name means nothing locally.

These are places to look, not evidence that anything has been filed. Links were checked on 2026-08-21; search links are queries, not records. What to ask for.
