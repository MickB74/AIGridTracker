What has happened so far (19 events)

  • 2024-09-04protest
    Pennsylvania anti-nuclear activists began organizing to oppose the restart, citing safety and evacuation concerns tied to the 1979 TMI-2 accident. · source
  • 2024-09-20announcement
    Constellation Energy announced a 20-year power purchase agreement with Microsoft to restart the 835 MW Three Mile Island Unit 1, renamed the Crane Clean Energy Center, to supply carbon-free power for Microsoft data centers; the deal is expected to create ~3,400 jobs and add $16 billion to Pennsylvania GDP. · source
  • 2024-09-25coverage
    Local reporting captured mixed community reactions in Londonderry Township/Dauphin County, with labor groups voicing support while some residents raised fear of another accident. · source
  • 2024-10-25hearing
    Constellation laid out its reopening timeline at a public hearing, telling regulators and residents it aimed to have Unit 1 producing power again by 2027-2028. · source
  • 2024-11-04filing
    Constellation submitted its initial restart application to the Nuclear Regulatory Commission for Three Mile Island Unit 1 (Crane Clean Energy Center). · source
  • 2025-03-20environmental
    Constellation began evaluating the environmental impacts of the restart as part of the NRC review process, covering water use from the Susquehanna River, air quality, and spent fuel management. · source
  • 2025-04-03lawsuit
    Activist Gene Stilp sued Dauphin County commissioners and the Public Safety Department, alleging misuse of taxpayer funds to promote the plant restart. · source
  • 2025-05-06filing
    The NRC formally accepted Constellation restart request for Three Mile Island Unit 1 for detailed technical review. · source
  • 2025-08-01public-comment

  • 2025-08-06hearing

  • 2025-08-15filing

  • 2025-09-15filing

Find the paper trail

Find the paper trail (0 filed · 11 places to look)

State environmental permits

Backup generators, water withdrawal and stormwater are permitted by the state, usually earlier and in more detail than anything filed locally.

  • Pennsylvania DEPThe program page: which permits this state requires, and the office that issues them.
  • DEP Data Center Permit Tracker (the only state register built for data centers — it already feeds this tracker)Searchable public filings — search the operator, the shell LLC on the deed, and the address.

County & town filings

The rezoning application, site plan, traffic and noise studies, staff report and every revision.

  • Search Londonderry Township (Dauphin County) planning filingssearchPlanning departments post application files under many different names. Find the department, then ask the clerk for the application number — that number unlocks everything else.
  • Search Londonderry Township (Dauphin County) property records / GISsearchThe parcel record names the buying LLC and the sale date, which is what ties an anonymous filing to an operator.

Utility & grid records

The electricity request is filed with the grid operator and the utility long before the town hears about it — and states the real load.

  • PJM Interconnection (Mid-Atlantic, Ohio Valley) — interconnection queueRequested megawatts, queue position and target in-service date. The MW here is the real number; the one at the town meeting is often phase one.
  • Pennsylvania Public Utility Commission — docket searchSpecial contracts, large-load tariffs and rate cases are docketed here. Testimony and exhibits are public, and are usually more candid than the press release.
  • FERC eLibraryFederal filings: transmission agreements, interconnection service agreements, and co-location disputes.
  • Search the serving utility for a will-serve lettersearchThe will-serve or load-service letter is the utility's own written commitment. Ask the utility and the PUC for it by name — it is frequently the first document to exist.

Federal databases

Works anywhere, and covers the operator's record at its other sites.

  • EPA ECHO facility searchEvery federally-reported air, water and waste permit at a facility, plus its inspection and violation history. Search by address or draw a radius around the site.
  • EPA EnvirofactsThe same underlying records, queryable by county — useful for 'what else is permitted near this site' rather than one facility.
  • EPA Facility Registry ServiceResolves the shell LLC on a permit to the facility it belongs to. Worth a look when the filing name means nothing locally.

These are places to look, not evidence that anything has been filed. Links were checked on 2026-08-21; search links are queries, not records. What to ask for.

A working set of identified proposals a community can track, not a census — most of the country's activity is not in here yet. Pennsylvania is the exception: its rows are synced from PA DEP's own data-center permit tracker, so the state's coverage is close to complete and carries the permit trail with it. **Each row carries its own provenance.** Rows showing a verification date link to reporting or the governing body's own record and were read on that date; rows marked *unverified* have no source on record — a lead to check, not a fact to cite. **Stage is derived, not asserted.** *Hearing scheduled*, *Awaiting decision*, *Approved* and *Withdrawn* are computed from each row's milestone dates on every rebuild, so a hearing date that has passed stops reading 'scheduled'. Hearings get moved and projects re-file under new names — treat a scheduled date as the earliest to act by, and confirm with the clerk before relying on it.
