What has happened so far (12 events)

  • 2019-04-01rezoning
    Mesa rezoned approximately 187 acres of Morrison family farmland at Elliot and Sossaman roads for the data center project, then known by the codename Project Red Hawk, in April 2019. · source
  • 2019-07-01vote
    Mesa City Council approved a development agreement and 25-year Government Property Lease Excise Tax (GPLET) incentive for Google's $1 billion data center on 187 acres at Elliot and Sossaman roads; the deal was negotiated under the codename Project Red Hawk with Google operating as Stone Applications LLC. · source
  • 2019-07-02coverage
    AZBEX reported the council approval and revealed Google as the company behind Project Red Hawk, noting the project had been secret for over a year due to a confidentiality agreement; Mayor Giles said the corridor would be anchored by Apple and Google. · source
  • 2023-06-14permit
    Mesa Design Review Board began reviewing the Phase 1 site plan for the 288,530-square-foot data center building, utility switchyard, medium-voltage substation, security kiosk, and employee office building; engineering firm HDR Inc. submitted the plans on behalf of Stone Applications LLC. · source
  • 2023-09-06construction
    Google broke ground on its $600 million Phase 1 data center in Mesa, its first physical presence in Arizona; Governor Katie Hobbs, Mayor John Giles, and Rep. Greg Stanton attended; Google announced the facility would be 100% air-cooled and donated $150,000 to Salt River Project for watershed restoration. · source
  • 2023-09-08coverage
    Construction Dive reported on Google's $600 million Mesa data center groundbreaking, noting it was Google's first physical presence in Arizona and that a new Google Cloud region was also coming to Phoenix. · source
  • 2023-09-10coverage
    Mesa Tribune reported that city officials were looking to slow data center growth, with Councilman Scott Somers saying Mesa had seen 'more than our fair share' of data centers; at least 15 new data center projects were reportedly eyeing Mesa, and Google's $600 million facility was noted alongside the city's concerns about low job creation per acre. · source
  • 2024-04-09hearing
    Mesa Design Review Board heard informational presentations on Google's Phase 2 data center (280,000 sq ft, air-cooled, codenamed Project Redhawk) and a separate one-story Redhawk HUB office building near Elliot and Sossaman roads; no formal action was taken. · source
  • 2024-04-09coverage
    ABC15 reported that Google's Phase 2 design review was underway for a 280,000-square-foot building mirroring Phase 1, plus a Redhawk-HUB office building, on the campus that now represented a total $1 billion investment on over 185 acres. · source
  • 2025-07-01operational
    Google's Phase 1 data center (288,530 sq ft) at the Red Hawk campus in Mesa became operational in July 2025, powered by air-cooling architecture with no water-intensive cooling towers. · source
  • 2025-07-08rezoning
    Mesa City Council adopted new data center zoning regulations establishing 'Data Center' as a distinct land use category, after 15 data centers were constructed, approved, or proposed in the city over six years; Vice Mayor Somers called it the first step in more objective design standards. · source
  • 2025-10-14hearing
    Mesa Design Review Board examined Phase 3 plans for a 283,500-square-foot data center building at 35 feet 10 inches tall with two server halls, including alternative compliance requests for wall and roof articulations under the Mesa Zoning Ordinance. · source

County & town filings

The rezoning application, site plan, traffic and noise studies, staff report and every revision.

  • Search Mesa planning filingssearchPlanning departments post application files under many different names. Find the department, then ask the clerk for the application number — that number unlocks everything else.
  • Search Mesa property records / GISsearchThe parcel record names the buying LLC and the sale date, which is what ties an anonymous filing to an operator.

Utility & grid records

The electricity request is filed with the grid operator and the utility long before the town hears about it — and states the real load.

  • No organised market covers this statenoteThere is no public queue to read. The record is the utility's integrated resource plan and its filings at the PUC — plus FERC eLibrary for anything federally docketed.
  • Arizona Corporation Commission — docket searchSpecial contracts, large-load tariffs and rate cases are docketed here. Testimony and exhibits are public, and are usually more candid than the press release.
  • FERC eLibraryFederal filings: transmission agreements, interconnection service agreements, and co-location disputes.
  • Search the serving utility for a will-serve lettersearchThe will-serve or load-service letter is the utility's own written commitment. Ask the utility and the PUC for it by name — it is frequently the first document to exist.

Federal databases

Works anywhere, and covers the operator's record at its other sites.

  • EPA ECHO facility searchEvery federally-reported air, water and waste permit at a facility, plus its inspection and violation history. Search by address or draw a radius around the site. This project's locality centres on 33.420, -111.830 — search that point with a 5-mile radius.
  • EPA EnvirofactsThe same underlying records, queryable by county — useful for 'what else is permitted near this site' rather than one facility.
  • EPA Facility Registry ServiceResolves the shell LLC on a permit to the facility it belongs to. Worth a look when the filing name means nothing locally.
