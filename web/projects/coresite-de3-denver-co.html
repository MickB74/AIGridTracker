CoreSite DE3 (Denver) Under construction

170,000 sq ft facility at 5050 N. Race St drawing 65-75MW. Under construction with no required community notice; opposition from the neighborhood was the direct catalyst for Denver's citywide one-year data center moratorium passed in May 2026.

What has happened so far (16 events)

  • 2022-11-10financing
    CoreSite purchased the Elyria-Swansea site (former concrete plant, heavy-industrial zoned) at Race Street and E. 49th Avenue for $33.5 million. · source
  • 2024-08-01filing
    Mayor Johnston's Denver Economic Development office brought CoreSite's $9M tax incentive request to City Council, which would have returned half of sales/use taxes on the project. · source
  • 2024-10-01withdrawal
    CoreSite dropped its pursuit of the $9M city tax break after council members raised concerns about water and energy usage; the company proceeded to build DE3 using by-right industrial zoning without further council review. · source
  • 2025-02-01report
    A state-funded community health study found Globeville-Elyria-Swansea residents have significantly higher asthma hospitalization rates than the Denver average, underscoring the neighborhood's existing pollution burden. · source
  • 2025-07-01permit
    Colorado DPHE issued an air quality permit allowing CoreSite to install 14 Kohler diesel backup generators (5,040 hp each) at DE3, capped at 25 hours/year of emergency or testing use. · source
  • 2025-09-01construction
    Construction on CoreSite's DE3 data center building was actively underway in Elyria-Swansea, part of a planned 600,000 sq ft, three-building, 60MW campus at 5050 N. Race St. · source
  • 2025-10-06construction
    CoreSite marked a 'topping out' milestone for the 180,000 sq ft DE3 building, the first purpose-built colocation data center in Denver in two decades. · source
  • 2025-10-14coverage
    Colorado Public Radio reported on whether the AI boom threatens local air quality in north Denver, detailing the CoreSite DE3 project, its generators, and neighborhood pollution concerns. · source
  • 2026-02-24protest
    Hundreds of GES residents attended a town hall on the CoreSite data center; CoreSite itself skipped the meeting citing 'safety concerns,' leaving Councilmember Watson, Xcel Energy, Denver Water and state health officials to face residents' questions. · source
  • 2026-03-12utility
    Colorado Sun reported that Xcel Energy's data-center-driven building boom threatens to shock consumers with higher electric bills, adding pressure for state action. · source
  • 2026-04-07utility

  • 2026-04-22public-comment

  • 2026-05-12filing

  • 2026-05-18moratorium

  • 2026-05-21moratorium

State environmental permits

Backup generators, water withdrawal and stormwater are permitted by the state, usually earlier and in more detail than anything filed locally.

  • Colorado CDPHE — Air Pollution Control DivisionThe program page: which permits this state requires, and the office that issues them.
  • Search Colorado CDPHE — Air Pollution Control Division for this projectsearchThis state publishes no searchable permit database, so a written records request to the agency is the reliable route. Use this search only to find the right office.

County & town filings

The rezoning application, site plan, traffic and noise studies, staff report and every revision.

  • Search Globeville-Elyria-Swansea (Denver) planning filingssearchPlanning departments post application files under many different names. Find the department, then ask the clerk for the application number — that number unlocks everything else.
  • Search Globeville-Elyria-Swansea (Denver) property records / GISsearchThe parcel record names the buying LLC and the sale date, which is what ties an anonymous filing to an operator.

Utility & grid records

The electricity request is filed with the grid operator and the utility long before the town hears about it — and states the real load.

  • No organised market covers this statenoteThere is no public queue to read. The record is the utility's integrated resource plan and its filings at the PUC — plus FERC eLibrary for anything federally docketed.
  • Colorado Public Utilities Commission — docket searchSpecial contracts, large-load tariffs and rate cases are docketed here. Testimony and exhibits are public, and are usually more candid than the press release.
  • FERC eLibraryFederal filings: transmission agreements, interconnection service agreements, and co-location disputes.
  • Search the serving utility for a will-serve lettersearchThe will-serve or load-service letter is the utility's own written commitment. Ask the utility and the PUC for it by name — it is frequently the first document to exist.

Federal databases

Works anywhere, and covers the operator's record at its other sites.

  • EPA ECHO facility searchEvery federally-reported air, water and waste permit at a facility, plus its inspection and violation history. Search by address or draw a radius around the site.
  • EPA EnvirofactsThe same underlying records, queryable by county — useful for 'what else is permitted near this site' rather than one facility.
  • EPA Facility Registry ServiceResolves the shell LLC on a permit to the facility it belongs to. Worth a look when the filing name means nothing locally.
