What has happened so far (16 events)

  • 2025-01-21rezoning
    Port Washington's Common Council took up annexing and rezoning roughly one-third of the 1,900-acre project site from the Town of Port Washington for a data center then tied to Houston developer Cloverleaf, with town board and council meetings held that day on about 2,000 acres (~17% of the town). · source
  • 2025-06-11announcement
    Vantage Data Centers was identified as the operator for Port Washington's proposed data center campus, described as a 3.5-gigawatt project with a 1.3-gigawatt first phase targeted for 2027. · source
  • 2025-07-18filing
    A Vantage official expressed concern in an email about the Wisconsin DNR's position that a full Environmental Impact Statement would be required for the Port Washington data center, according to records later cited in litigation. · source
  • 2025-08-20vote
    The Port Washington Common Council unanimously approved a development agreement with Vantage Data Centers for an $8 billion, four-building data center campus and approved annexing nearly 700 more acres, bringing the total annexed to 1,315 of the 1,900-acre site. · source
  • 2025-09-23filing
    A Vantage official texted the Port Washington mayor about the governor's office questioning why an Environmental Impact Statement would 'kill the project,' according to records cited in the Sierra Club/MEA lawsuit. · source
  • 2025-10-22announcement
    OpenAI, Oracle and Vantage Data Centers announced the 'Lighthouse' Stargate data center campus in Port Washington: a $15 billion investment for four data centers on 672 acres delivering close to a gigawatt of AI capacity, with a minimum $175 million in regional infrastructure upgrades and completion scheduled for 2028. · source
  • 2025-12-01protest
    Three members of the opposition group Great Lakes Neighbors United were arrested at a Port Washington city council meeting in December 2025, according to later reporting. · source
  • 2025-12-18construction
    Vantage held a groundbreaking ceremony marking the start of construction on the $15 billion Lighthouse data center campus in Port Washington, with the first phase to be completed in 2028. · source
  • 2026-01-29lawsuit
    The Metropolitan Milwaukee Association of Commerce (MMAC) sued the City of Port Washington seeking an injunction to block an April referendum that would require voter approval for TID-financed projects exceeding $10 million. · source
  • 2026-02-13lawsuit
    Great Lakes Neighbors United's lawsuit challenging the city's tax incremental district was reported, contending the TID provides property tax reimbursement and interest payments to the developer totaling more than $458 million over 18 years; the city moved to dismiss. · source
  • 2026-02-16withdrawal
    A recall petition against Port Washington Mayor Ted Neitzke, organized by Great Lakes Neighbors United, was submitted by the February 16 deadline but the city's initial review found it fell several hundred signatures short of the roughly 1,600-1,800 required. · source
  • 2026-02-24ruling
    A court denied MMAC's request to halt the Port Washington tax-incentive referendum, allowing the April ballot measure to proceed. · source
  • 2026-03-15protest
    Port Washington neighbors pushed back on 24-hour weekday construction at Vantage's Lighthouse campus, submitting a 100-person petition to restore original hours and citing round-the-clock work lights and backup beeping, with the Planning Commission set to take up the issue. · source
  • 2026-04-08vote
    Port Washington voters passed a referendum with 66% approval requiring voter approval before the city grants tax incentives exceeding $10 million to developers; organizers said the measure does not affect the current Vantage deal but restricts future data center tax breaks. · source
  • 2026-06-12permit
    The Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources issued permits for the Vantage Port Washington data center project without preparing a full Environmental Impact Statement. · source
  • 2026-07-10lawsuit
    Midwest Environmental Advocates, on behalf of Sierra Club Wisconsin, sued the Wisconsin DNR in Ozaukee County Circuit Court, alleging the agency violated the Wisconsin Environmental Policy Act by issuing permits for the data center without a full Environmental Impact Statement. · source

Utility & grid records

The electricity request is filed with the grid operator and the utility long before the town hears about it — and states the real load.

  • MISO (Midwest & Gulf) — interconnection queueRequested megawatts, queue position and target in-service date. The MW here is the real number; the one at the town meeting is often phase one.
  • Public Service Commission of Wisconsin — docket searchSpecial contracts, large-load tariffs and rate cases are docketed here. Testimony and exhibits are public, and are usually more candid than the press release.
  • FERC eLibraryFederal filings: transmission agreements, interconnection service agreements, and co-location disputes.
  • Search the serving utility for a will-serve lettersearchThe will-serve or load-service letter is the utility's own written commitment. Ask the utility and the PUC for it by name — it is frequently the first document to exist.

Federal databases

Works anywhere, and covers the operator's record at its other sites.

  • EPA ECHO facility searchEvery federally-reported air, water and waste permit at a facility, plus its inspection and violation history. Search by address or draw a radius around the site. This project's locality centres on 43.390, -87.870 — search that point with a 5-mile radius.
  • EPA EnvirofactsThe same underlying records, queryable by county — useful for 'what else is permitted near this site' rather than one facility.
  • EPA Facility Registry ServiceResolves the shell LLC on a permit to the facility it belongs to. Worth a look when the filing name means nothing locally.
