What has happened so far (12 events)

  • 2025-11-01filing
    Stak Energy submitted its initial application to Alaska DNR Division of Oil and Gas for a 50-year state land lease (ADL 422741) covering 715.4 acres off the Dalton Highway near Milepost 390 for a natural-gas-powered AI data center campus; DNR solicited competing bids and none came in. · source
  • 2026-03-12filing
    Stak Energy filed a revised application amending its original lease submission to the Division of Oil and Gas. · source
  • 2026-05-12ruling
    Alaska DNR issued a preliminary best-interest finding for the proposed 50-year land lease to Stak Energy, granting preliminary approval for the 715-acre parcel. · source
  • 2026-05-14coverage
    Alaska Beacon broke the story of the proposed $500 million, up-to-3GW natural-gas-powered Stak Energy data center campus on the North Slope. · source
  • 2026-05-18coverage
    Anchorage Daily News expanded coverage detailing the 715-acre footprint, dry-air cooling advantage, and lack of a firm natural-gas supply deal at time of filing. · source
  • 2026-05-26coverage
    Alaska Public Media reported on the proposal, noting Stak Energy Alaska roots and staffing plans. · source
  • 2026-05-28report
    Northern Alaska Environmental Center published an analysis of the lease documents identifying unresolved risks: incomplete wetland delineation, no permafrost thermal modeling, no bird/caribou surveys, undisclosed lease financial terms, and estimated gas consumption of 128-183 billion cubic feet per year. · source
  • 2026-06-01environmental
    The Alaska Center publicized environmental concerns including potential heat-island warming effects extending up to 6.2 miles, the need for 7 million cubic yards of gravel to raise the site 5-7 feet, and fuel consumption potentially exceeding twice urban Alaska electrical generation; urged residents to file public comments. · source
  • 2026-06-15public-comment
    Original public comment deadline on the DNR preliminary best-interest decision for the Stak Energy lease. · source
  • 2026-07-03protest
    Coverage reported Alaskans had flooded the state comment docket with opposition, including submissions reading "HELL NO! To ANY DATA CENTERS" and "NO AI," ahead of the extended comment deadline. · source
  • 2026-07-07report
    Anchorage Daily News reported more than 500 public comments submitted to DNR, with fewer than a dozen supporting the project and the vast majority opposed; Stak Energy characterized most as form letters. · source
  • 2026-07-17public-comment
    Extended deadline (4:30 p.m.) for public comments on the proposed 50-year land lease; DNR said it would assess comments before issuing a final lease decision. · source

Find the paper trail

Find the paper trail (0 filed · 11 places to look)

State environmental permits

Backup generators, water withdrawal and stormwater are permitted by the state, usually earlier and in more detail than anything filed locally.

  • Alaska DEC — Division of Air QualityThe program page: which permits this state requires, and the office that issues them.
  • Search Alaska DEC — Division of Air Quality for this projectsearchThis state publishes no searchable permit database, so a written records request to the agency is the reliable route. Use this search only to find the right office.

County & town filings

The rezoning application, site plan, traffic and noise studies, staff report and every revision.

  • Search North Slope Borough planning filingssearchPlanning departments post application files under many different names. Find the department, then ask the clerk for the application number — that number unlocks everything else.
  • Search North Slope Borough property records / GISsearchThe parcel record names the buying LLC and the sale date, which is what ties an anonymous filing to an operator.

Utility & grid records

The electricity request is filed with the grid operator and the utility long before the town hears about it — and states the real load.

  • No organised market covers this statenoteThere is no public queue to read. The record is the utility's integrated resource plan and its filings at the PUC — plus FERC eLibrary for anything federally docketed.
  • Regulatory Commission of Alaska — docket searchSpecial contracts, large-load tariffs and rate cases are docketed here. Testimony and exhibits are public, and are usually more candid than the press release.
  • FERC eLibraryFederal filings: transmission agreements, interconnection service agreements, and co-location disputes.
  • Search the serving utility for a will-serve lettersearchThe will-serve or load-service letter is the utility's own written commitment. Ask the utility and the PUC for it by name — it is frequently the first document to exist.

Federal databases

Works anywhere, and covers the operator's record at its other sites.

  • EPA ECHO facility searchEvery federally-reported air, water and waste permit at a facility, plus its inspection and violation history. Search by address or draw a radius around the site.
  • EPA EnvirofactsThe same underlying records, queryable by county — useful for 'what else is permitted near this site' rather than one facility.
  • EPA Facility Registry ServiceResolves the shell LLC on a permit to the facility it belongs to. Worth a look when the filing name means nothing locally.

These are places to look, not evidence that anything has been filed. Links were checked on 2026-08-21; search links are queries, not records. What to ask for.
