What has happened so far (13 events)

  • 2018-12-01announcement
    In December 2018, Google affiliate Montauk Innovations LLC announced it was considering New Albany for a $600 million data center and acquired over 440 acres in Franklin and Licking counties for $54.5 million. · source
  • 2018-12-01incentive
    In December 2018, the Ohio Tax Credit Authority approved a 100%, 15-year sales tax exemption for Google's Montauk Innovations data center project, valued at approximately $43.5 million over the life of the credit and renewable for up to 40 years. · source
  • 2019-11-01construction
    Holder Construction broke ground on November 1, 2019 on Google's $600 million data center on 440 acres in New Albany, part of Google's $13 billion 2019 expansion plan for seven new U.S. data centers. · source
  • 2019-11-01incentive
    New Albany's incentive package for Google included 100% property tax abatement for 15 years and a waiver of up to $250,000 in building and permitting fees, with a minimum annual revenue generation requirement of $750,000 beginning in 2021. · source
  • 2021-01-01operational
    Google's New Albany data center was completed and became operational in 2021, according to property records listing the year built as 2021. · source
  • 2021-08-20announcement
    On August 20, 2021, Google announced an additional $1 billion investment to expand its New Albany data center campus and disclosed it had acquired 618 more acres (500 in Columbus, approximately 120 in Lancaster) for potential future data centers, bringing total Ohio acreage to over 1,000. · source
  • 2022-05-24operational
    Google launched its Columbus Ohio cloud region (us-east5) on May 24, 2022, its 2nd Midwest region, 10th in North America, and 33rd globally, with three availability zones served by the New Albany data center infrastructure. · source
  • 2023-05-04announcement
    On May 4, 2023, Google announced it would build two additional data centers in Columbus and Lancaster, Ohio, expanding beyond its existing New Albany campus to create a trio of Central Ohio facilities. · source
  • 2023-08-29financing
    On August 29, 2023, Google announced a $1.7 billion investment to support its three Central Ohio data center campuses in New Albany, Columbus, and Lancaster, bringing cumulative Ohio investment to over $3.7 billion; each facility generates roughly 1,200 construction jobs and several hundred permanent positions. · source
  • 2024-06-18financing
    On June 18, 2024, Google announced an additional $2.3 billion investment across its Columbus, New Albany, and Lancaster data center sites, bringing cumulative Ohio spending to over $4.4 billion since 2019. · source
  • 2025-05-01filing
    In May 2025, Google subsidiary Montauk Innovations acquired an additional 85-acre parcel on Harrison Road Northwest in New Albany's business park in Licking County for $63 million. · source
  • 2025-11-12utility
    On November 12, 2025, TotalEnergies announced a 15-year power purchase agreement to supply Google's Ohio data centers with 1.5 TWh of certified renewable electricity from the Montpelier solar farm in Ohio, connected to the PJM grid. · source
  • 2026-04-02public-comment
    On April 2, 2026, the Ohio Ballot Board unanimously approved a petition drive for a constitutional amendment to ban new data centers consuming over 25 MW monthly, requiring 400,000+ signatures by July 2026; the campaign cited New Albany-area data center impacts including rising electric rates and farmland loss. · source

State environmental permits

Backup generators, water withdrawal and stormwater are permitted by the state, usually earlier and in more detail than anything filed locally.

  • Ohio EPA — Division of Air Pollution ControlThe program page: which permits this state requires, and the office that issues them.
  • Ohio EPA eBusiness Center (Air Services)Searchable public filings — search the operator, the shell LLC on the deed, and the address.

County & town filings

The rezoning application, site plan, traffic and noise studies, staff report and every revision.

  • Search New Albany planning filingssearchPlanning departments post application files under many different names. Find the department, then ask the clerk for the application number — that number unlocks everything else.
  • Search New Albany property records / GISsearchThe parcel record names the buying LLC and the sale date, which is what ties an anonymous filing to an operator.

Utility & grid records

The electricity request is filed with the grid operator and the utility long before the town hears about it — and states the real load.

  • PJM Interconnection (Mid-Atlantic, Ohio Valley) — interconnection queueRequested megawatts, queue position and target in-service date. The MW here is the real number; the one at the town meeting is often phase one.
  • Public Utilities Commission of Ohio — docket searchSpecial contracts, large-load tariffs and rate cases are docketed here. Testimony and exhibits are public, and are usually more candid than the press release.
  • FERC eLibraryFederal filings: transmission agreements, interconnection service agreements, and co-location disputes.
  • Search the serving utility for a will-serve lettersearchThe will-serve or load-service letter is the utility's own written commitment. Ask the utility and the PUC for it by name — it is frequently the first document to exist.

Federal databases

Works anywhere, and covers the operator's record at its other sites.

  • EPA ECHO facility searchEvery federally-reported air, water and waste permit at a facility, plus its inspection and violation history. Search by address or draw a radius around the site. This project's locality centres on 40.080, -82.790 — search that point with a 5-mile radius.
  • EPA EnvirofactsThe same underlying records, queryable by county — useful for 'what else is permitted near this site' rather than one facility.
  • EPA Facility Registry ServiceResolves the shell LLC on a permit to the facility it belongs to. Worth a look when the filing name means nothing locally.
