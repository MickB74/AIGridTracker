Project dossier · Fulton County (Akron), Indiana

Decennial Group Akron Data Center Campus (proposed) Blocked by ban

Decennial Group co-founder David Pavlick (also spelled 'Pavlik' in some coverage) proposed a roughly 500 MW, 300-acre hyperscale data center campus near Akron, in Fulton County, no specific parcel/site was ever named publicly, and no hyperscale tenant was named (Pavlick said an unnamed hyperscale company larger than Decennial's existing CoreWeave-tenanted Hammond, IN facility was interested). Estimated $100-150 million in associated infrastructure investment (roads, bridges, community water upgrades) plus a closed-loop water design were described at a Feb. 4, 2026 public open house at the Akron Banquet Hall attended by 40+ residents; reporting says commissioners themselves learned of the proposal only about 10 days before that open house. The Fulton County Area Plan Commission recommended a 12-month moratorium 6-1 on Feb. 10, 2026 specifically in response to this proposal, and the Board of Commissioners adopted it 2-1 on March 2, 2026, effectively pausing the project. No later announcement of the project being formally withdrawn was located; it is currently blocked by the moratorium rather than confirmed dead.

State environmental permits

Backup generators, water withdrawal and stormwater are permitted by the state, usually earlier and in more detail than anything filed locally.

  • IDEM Virtual File CabinetSearchable public filings — search the operator, the shell LLC on the deed, and the address.

County & town filings

The rezoning application, site plan, traffic and noise studies, staff report and every revision.

  • Search Fulton County (Akron) planning filingssearchPlanning departments post application files under many different names. Find the department, then ask the clerk for the application number — that number unlocks everything else.
  • Search Fulton County (Akron) property records / GISsearchThe parcel record names the buying LLC and the sale date, which is what ties an anonymous filing to an operator.

Utility & grid records

The electricity request is filed with the grid operator and the utility long before the town hears about it — and states the real load.

  • MISO (Midwest & Gulf) — interconnection queueRequested megawatts, queue position and target in-service date. The MW here is the real number; the one at the town meeting is often phase one.
  • PJM Interconnection (Mid-Atlantic, Ohio Valley) — interconnection queueRequested megawatts, queue position and target in-service date. The MW here is the real number; the one at the town meeting is often phase one.
  • Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission — docket searchSpecial contracts, large-load tariffs and rate cases are docketed here. Testimony and exhibits are public, and are usually more candid than the press release.
  • FERC eLibraryFederal filings: transmission agreements, interconnection service agreements, and co-location disputes.
  • Search the serving utility for a will-serve lettersearchThe will-serve or load-service letter is the utility's own written commitment. Ask the utility and the PUC for it by name — it is frequently the first document to exist.

Federal databases

Works anywhere, and covers the operator's record at its other sites.

  • EPA ECHO facility searchEvery federally-reported air, water and waste permit at a facility, plus its inspection and violation history. Search by address or draw a radius around the site. This project's locality centres on 41.041, -86.016 — search that point with a 5-mile radius.
  • EPA EnvirofactsThe same underlying records, queryable by county — useful for 'what else is permitted near this site' rather than one facility.
  • EPA Facility Registry ServiceResolves the shell LLC on a permit to the facility it belongs to. Worth a look when the filing name means nothing locally.
