What has happened so far (11 events)

  • 2024-06-13filing
    DataBank registered its first Red Oak building, DFW9 (425,170 sq ft, two-story data center with office space, $256M estimated cost), with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TABS project TABS2024020821). · source
  • 2024-09-10announcement
    DataBank announced development of a 480MW, 292-acre, eight-building data center campus in Red Oak, Ellis County, Texas (its third new-campus announcement in 12 months), with Phase 1 covering four buildings and a 400MW Oncor substation delivering 240MW of critical IT power; initial ready-for-service targeted for Q2 2026. · source
  • 2024-11-05filing
    DataBank filed plans for a second Red Oak building, DFW10 ($256M, 425,000+ sq ft), with construction slated to begin February 2025 and complete by mid-2028; report described it as a $256M addition to the growing Red Oak campus. · source
  • 2024-11-05filing
    Dallas Express reported DataBank's $265M expansion of the Red Oak campus (second facility), a 425,000 sq ft two-story building designed by JHET Architects, with construction beginning February 2025 and completion expected July 2028; full build-out envisions eight buildings totaling 3.4M+ sq ft. · source
  • 2025-12-01construction
    Construction work on DFW11 at the Red Oak campus began (per reported December 2025–January 2028 build schedule), part of the campus's staggered build-out following DFW9 and DFW10. · source
  • 2026-04-21financing
    DataBank closed a $2.0 billion construction loan — its largest financing to date, led by MUFG Bank under DataBank's Green Financing Framework — to fund the first three Red Oak buildings (DFW9, DFW10, DFW11: 600,000 sq ft, 180MW), all fully leased to an AI/cloud tenant (reported to be Oracle); the financing was said to accelerate delivery by about 18 months. · source
  • 2026-05-11hearing
    Red Oak Planning & Zoning Commission and City Council held a separate, contentious public hearing on an unrelated 830-acre Compass Datacenters project elsewhere in Red Oak; over 150 residents attended amid a 1,600-signature opposition petition citing noise, water, traffic and power-grid concerns (noted here for context but distinct from DataBank's Stainback Road campus). · source
  • 2026-06-01construction
    Reporting described DataBank's Red Oak campus in active construction, with initial concrete pours underway and electrical systems installation beginning; residents voiced mixed reactions including concerns about noise and water use, while DataBank held public meetings and appointed a community liaison. · source
  • 2026-06-16financing
    DataBank closed $1.45 billion in new financing across two transactions: an $800M corporate revolving credit facility and a $650M upsize (including DataBank's first private-placement notes) of the Red Oak construction loan to fund a fourth building (60MW additional IT capacity), bringing total Red Oak campus financing to $2.65 billion. · source
  • 2026-08-01construction
    Dallas Morning News reported DFW9 was expected to come online in August 2026, with DFW10 (interior work, ~$301M, completion January 2027) and DFW11 (~$315M, completion March 2027) following on a staggered schedule; residents cited concerns about scale, infrastructure strain, a 10-year tax-abatement delay on community benefits, and limited local job creation relative to project size. · source
  • 2026-08-11moratorium
    Texas Governor Greg Abbott ordered a statewide audit of all data centers connecting to the ERCOT grid, effectively pausing new interconnections pending review — a state-level action affecting all Texas data center projects, including those on DataBank's Red Oak campus. · source

State environmental permits

Backup generators, water withdrawal and stormwater are permitted by the state, usually earlier and in more detail than anything filed locally.

  • Texas TCEQ — Air Permits DivisionThe program page: which permits this state requires, and the office that issues them.
  • TCEQ Central RegistrySearchable public filings — search the operator, the shell LLC on the deed, and the address.

County & town filings

The rezoning application, site plan, traffic and noise studies, staff report and every revision.

  • Search Red Oak (Ellis County) planning filingssearchPlanning departments post application files under many different names. Find the department, then ask the clerk for the application number — that number unlocks everything else.
  • Search Red Oak (Ellis County) property records / GISsearchThe parcel record names the buying LLC and the sale date, which is what ties an anonymous filing to an operator.

Utility & grid records

The electricity request is filed with the grid operator and the utility long before the town hears about it — and states the real load.

  • ERCOT (Texas) — interconnection queueRequested megawatts, queue position and target in-service date. The MW here is the real number; the one at the town meeting is often phase one.
  • Public Utility Commission of Texas — docket searchSpecial contracts, large-load tariffs and rate cases are docketed here. Testimony and exhibits are public, and are usually more candid than the press release.
  • FERC eLibraryFederal filings: transmission agreements, interconnection service agreements, and co-location disputes.
  • Search the serving utility for a will-serve lettersearchThe will-serve or load-service letter is the utility's own written commitment. Ask the utility and the PUC for it by name — it is frequently the first document to exist.

Federal databases

Works anywhere, and covers the operator's record at its other sites.

  • EPA ECHO facility searchEvery federally-reported air, water and waste permit at a facility, plus its inspection and violation history. Search by address or draw a radius around the site.
  • EPA EnvirofactsThe same underlying records, queryable by county — useful for 'what else is permitted near this site' rather than one facility.
  • EPA Facility Registry ServiceResolves the shell LLC on a permit to the facility it belongs to. Worth a look when the filing name means nothing locally.

These are places to look, not evidence that anything has been filed. Links were checked on 2026-08-21; search links are queries, not records. What to ask for.
