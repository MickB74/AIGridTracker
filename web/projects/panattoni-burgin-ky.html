What has happened so far (13 events)

  • 2025-03-27incentive
    Kentucky enacted HB 775, expanding sales-and-use tax exemptions for qualified data centers statewide (effective July 1, 2025). · source
  • 2026-02-18public-comment
    Over 200 residents attended a Mercer County Joint Planning and Zoning Commission meeting, an early public airing of concerns about Project Bluegrass impact on farmland, wellbeing, and resource consumption. · source
  • 2026-07-09vote
    Burgin City Council voted 3-2 to advance a first reading of an ordinance to annex more than 1,000 acres of farmland, roughly doubling the city size, to enable Panattoni proposed hyperscale data center to bypass Mercer County zoning. · source
  • 2026-07-16vote
    Mercer County Fiscal Court voted to begin drafting an ordinance establishing a moratorium on new data center construction in the county. · source
  • 2026-07-20withdrawal
    Burgin canceled a scheduled special city council meeting on the annexation ordinance amid mounting public opposition and objections from neighboring Harrodsburg over water-supply capacity. · source
  • 2026-07-24public-comment
    Mercer County Joint Planning and Zoning Commission held a roughly two-hour public comment session on a proposed 17-page county data-center ordinance that would cap data-center zoning at 1,500 acres countywide. · source
  • 2026-07-30hearing
    Mercer County Fiscal Court gave first reading to the data-center moratorium ordinance; a related follow-up public meeting was held at Campbellsville University Harrodsburg Athletic Center. · source
  • 2026-08-06ruling
    Governor Andy Beshear signed Executive Order 2026-494, requiring data-center developers statewide to submit energy plans protecting ratepayers, pay their full share of taxes, and directing state agencies to deny permits causing negative environmental impacts. · source
  • 2026-08-12moratorium
    Mercer County Fiscal Court unanimously passed a one-year moratorium on new data-center applications in unincorporated parts of the county — a moratorium that would not apply if the project site is annexed into Burgin. · source
  • 2026-08-13public-comment
    Burgin City Council held its first public-comment session on the data center; 14 residents spoke against the project (an overflow room was needed) and the council deferred the annexation vote. · source
  • 2026-08-13report
    Reporting described Project Bluegrass as a proposed four-building, 900-acre data center campus up to 1,300 MW, with the developer claiming 1,500+ construction jobs, 100 permanent jobs, and $25-30 million in projected annual tax revenue for local schools. · source
  • 2026-08-17coverage
    Statewide public-radio reporting placed the Burgin/Mercer County fight in the context of more than a dozen Kentucky data-center proposals since the 2025 tax-incentive law, alongside moratoriums in Oldham County and Louisville Metro. · source
  • 2026-08-18coverage
    Kentucky Lantern analyzed whether Beshear data-center executive order has legal teeth absent enabling state legislation, in the context of the Burgin/Mercer County fight. · source

State environmental permits

Backup generators, water withdrawal and stormwater are permitted by the state, usually earlier and in more detail than anything filed locally.

  • Kentucky Division for Air QualityThe program page: which permits this state requires, and the office that issues them.
  • Search Kentucky Division for Air Quality for this projectsearchThis state publishes no searchable permit database, so a written records request to the agency is the reliable route. Use this search only to find the right office.

County & town filings

The rezoning application, site plan, traffic and noise studies, staff report and every revision.

  • Search Mercer County / Burgin planning filingssearchPlanning departments post application files under many different names. Find the department, then ask the clerk for the application number — that number unlocks everything else.
  • Search Mercer County / Burgin property records / GISsearchThe parcel record names the buying LLC and the sale date, which is what ties an anonymous filing to an operator.

Utility & grid records

The electricity request is filed with the grid operator and the utility long before the town hears about it — and states the real load.

  • MISO (Midwest & Gulf) — interconnection queueRequested megawatts, queue position and target in-service date. The MW here is the real number; the one at the town meeting is often phase one.
  • PJM Interconnection (Mid-Atlantic, Ohio Valley) — interconnection queueRequested megawatts, queue position and target in-service date. The MW here is the real number; the one at the town meeting is often phase one.
  • Kentucky Public Service Commission — docket searchSpecial contracts, large-load tariffs and rate cases are docketed here. Testimony and exhibits are public, and are usually more candid than the press release.
  • FERC eLibraryFederal filings: transmission agreements, interconnection service agreements, and co-location disputes.
  • Search the serving utility for a will-serve lettersearchThe will-serve or load-service letter is the utility's own written commitment. Ask the utility and the PUC for it by name — it is frequently the first document to exist.

Federal databases

Works anywhere, and covers the operator's record at its other sites.

  • EPA ECHO facility searchEvery federally-reported air, water and waste permit at a facility, plus its inspection and violation history. Search by address or draw a radius around the site.
  • EPA EnvirofactsThe same underlying records, queryable by county — useful for 'what else is permitted near this site' rather than one facility.
  • EPA Facility Registry ServiceResolves the shell LLC on a permit to the facility it belongs to. Worth a look when the filing name means nothing locally.

These are places to look, not evidence that anything has been filed. Links were checked on 2026-08-21; search links are queries, not records. What to ask for.
