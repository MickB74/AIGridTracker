What has happened so far (10 events)

  • 2019-10-04announcement
    Google held a groundbreaking ceremony for its first Nebraska data center in Papillion, confirming a $600 million investment; Governor Pete Ricketts attended. · source
  • 2022-04-21announcement
    Google announced a $750 million investment to build a new data center near State Street and Blair High Road in northwest Omaha, its first in the city proper, creating 120 jobs; Governor Ricketts and Mayor Stothert attended the event at the Papillion facility. · source
  • 2023-01-03rezoning
    Westwood Solutions LLC, representing Google, filed to rezone an additional 187 acres adjoining the existing 270-acre northwest Omaha site (totaling ~460 acres) for a data center campus of over 2.2 million gross square feet with 24/7 operations; the developer agreed to donate $100,000 for destruction of a 5-acre virgin prairie. · source
  • 2023-08-22announcement
    Google announced $1.2 billion in 2023 Nebraska investment, confirming a new $600 million Lincoln data center and Papillion expansion; the northwest Omaha campus was described as 'soon to be operational.' · source
  • 2024-11-25announcement
    Google reported $930 million in 2024 capital investment across its Nebraska data center campuses in Papillion, Omaha, and Lincoln, bringing its five-year cumulative Nebraska investment to over $4.4 billion. · source
  • 2026-03-13filing
    Flatwater Free Press reported Google proposed a new Nebraska data center requiring 1,000-3,000 MW (more than all of Lincoln's 800 MW peak demand), targeting 2029 online date; Tenaska optioned 2,600+ acres across Gage, Lancaster, Cass, and Otoe counties, and Governor Pillen introduced LB1261 to allow private energy generation for the project. · source
  • 2026-06-02vote
    Governor Pillen signed LB1261, permitting private companies to develop on-site electrical generation for industrial customers requiring over 1,000 MW, creating an exception to public power districts' exclusive authority; the law takes effect July 18, 2026. · source
  • 2026-06-03environmental
    Google awarded a $3 million grant to Omaha's Metropolitan Utilities District (MUD) to install acoustic leak-detection sensors across 500 miles of water lines, part of a $17 million nationwide water stewardship initiative; MUD projects the system could save up to 1 billion gallons annually. · source
  • 2026-07-08coverage
    Flatwater Free Press reported Google's Nebraska data centers consumed approximately 732 million gallons of water in 2025, up from 46.6 million gallons in 2022; new legislation LB1010 will require data center companies to disclose annual water consumption and power demand. · source
  • 2026-07-20incentive
    Governor Pillen signed an executive order suspending taxpayer-funded tax incentives for data centers and establishing a task force to identify safeguards protecting Nebraska's natural resources from data center operations. · source

Find the paper trail (0 filed · 11 places to look)

State environmental permits

Backup generators, water withdrawal and stormwater are permitted by the state, usually earlier and in more detail than anything filed locally.

  • Nebraska Dept. of Environment & EnergyThe program page: which permits this state requires, and the office that issues them.
  • Search Nebraska Dept. of Environment & Energy for this projectsearchThis state publishes no searchable permit database, so a written records request to the agency is the reliable route. Use this search only to find the right office.

County & town filings

The rezoning application, site plan, traffic and noise studies, staff report and every revision.

  • Search Omaha planning filingssearchPlanning departments post application files under many different names. Find the department, then ask the clerk for the application number — that number unlocks everything else.
  • Search Omaha property records / GISsearchThe parcel record names the buying LLC and the sale date, which is what ties an anonymous filing to an operator.

Utility & grid records

The electricity request is filed with the grid operator and the utility long before the town hears about it — and states the real load.

  • Southwest Power Pool (Plains) — interconnection queueRequested megawatts, queue position and target in-service date. The MW here is the real number; the one at the town meeting is often phase one.
  • Nebraska Power Review Board — docket searchSpecial contracts, large-load tariffs and rate cases are docketed here. Testimony and exhibits are public, and are usually more candid than the press release.
  • FERC eLibraryFederal filings: transmission agreements, interconnection service agreements, and co-location disputes.
  • Search the serving utility for a will-serve lettersearchThe will-serve or load-service letter is the utility's own written commitment. Ask the utility and the PUC for it by name — it is frequently the first document to exist.

Federal databases

Works anywhere, and covers the operator's record at its other sites.

  • EPA ECHO facility searchEvery federally-reported air, water and waste permit at a facility, plus its inspection and violation history. Search by address or draw a radius around the site. This project's locality centres on 41.160, -96.080 — search that point with a 5-mile radius.
  • EPA EnvirofactsThe same underlying records, queryable by county — useful for 'what else is permitted near this site' rather than one facility.
  • EPA Facility Registry ServiceResolves the shell LLC on a permit to the facility it belongs to. Worth a look when the filing name means nothing locally.

These are places to look, not evidence that anything has been filed. Links were checked on 2026-08-21; search links are queries, not records. What to ask for.
